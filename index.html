      Red flag 2 · Most dangerous

      Asking for your private details

      Why this is a warning sign

      No real bank, company or government office ever asks you to send your OTP, PIN, password, card number, CVV, Aadhaar or PAN in a message. With these, a scammer can empty your account or take over your WhatsApp. Don't reply, and never share them, even if the message looks official.

      Red flag 3 · Risky link

      A link or app you shouldn't open

      Why this is a warning sign

      Real banks and companies don't text you shortened links, links asking you to update KYC, verify or claim something, or tell you to download an app to fix a problem. These lead to fake pages that steal your details, or apps that let scammers control your phone.

      Don't click it. If you're worried, open the company's official app or type its website yourself.

      Red flag 1

      Pressure: they're trying to rush you

      Why this is a warning sign

      Scammers want you to act before you have time to think or ask someone. Real banks, delivery companies and government offices don't give you minutes to respond. If a message is rushing you, slow down, and check it yourself through a number or app you already trust.

      This tool catches common scams and tricks, but not every scam. Always be careful while sharing details and looking at random and scam-like messages in your messages app.

      How this checker works

      Paste a WhatsApp or SMS message and press Check message. It looks for the tricks scammers use most and gives the message a risk score from 0 to 100.

      What it checks

      • Asking for private details: requests to share your OTP, PIN, password, card number, CVV, Aadhaar or PAN. Messages that just give you an OTP, or warn you never to share it, are fine.
      • Risky links and apps: shortened links like bit.ly, links with words like KYC, verify or claim, and requests to install apps like AnyDesk or APK files.
      • Money tricks: prize and lottery wins, account-block and KYC threats, parcel fees, electricity cut-offs, work-from-home jobs, and unknown phone numbers to call.
      • Pressure: words that rush you, like "urgent", "immediately" or "within 24 hours".
      • Other warning signs: requests for payment, "Hi Mum, new number" messages, and police or "digital arrest" threats.

      Reading the score

      • Under 30: looks safe
      • 30 to 60: be careful
      • Above 60: likely a scam

      Each red flag adds points, and different tricks together add extra. Every flag shows the exact words spotted and why they matter.
